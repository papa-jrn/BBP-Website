## The situation

The Interactive Media Laboratory at Dartmouth Medical School spent years building
government-funded interactive training for clinicians and emergency responders — simulation-led
programs, funded by the NIH, the CDC and the federal Office on Violence Against Women, authored
and designed by Dr Joe Henderson.

In 2010 the lab closed. That is normally where federally funded software goes to die: the grant
ends, the lab winds down, and programs that cost millions to build stop being available to the
people they were built for.

## What happened instead

World 2 Systems was formed to take the programs to market — a technology transfer out of
Dartmouth that kept four clinical training products in active national use for another decade.

**Sexual Assault: Forensic and Clinical Management**, delivered with the International
Association of Forensic Nurses and the federal Office on Violence Against Women.

**The Virtual Terrorism Response Academy: Ops-Plus for WMD Hazmat**, used by fire, police, EMS,
military and federal trainers.

**Genetics in Clinical Practice: A Team Approach**, produced in collaboration with the CDC and
the American Medical Association, accredited for 12 AMA PRA Category 1 Credits, with a virtual
mini-fellowship led by Edward McCabe, MD, PhD.

**Smoking Cessation for Pregnancy and Beyond: A Virtual Clinic**, built around simulated
patients.

## The work

**All four product websites.** Each program needed its own site to explain a complex clinical
product to a sceptical professional audience, establish accreditation credibility, and sell. A
clinician deciding whether to spend twelve hours of continuing education credit on an
interactive program is making a serious professional judgement, and the site is where that
judgement gets made.

**Two of the programs themselves.** Beyond the marketing sites, the genetics and smoking
cessation programs were co-built with the IML team — Eric Kidd and Cecile Williams — under
Dr Henderson's authorship and design.

**The business behind it.** Payroll, tax and grant compliance, federal reporting and client
support for multimillion-dollar government contracts, plus front-line technical support for
nurses and physicians working through certification in the field.

> actually great fun to do. This program sets a very high bar for web-based education... or any
> continuing education. Highly recommended!"
>
> — **C. Everett Koop, MD, ScD**, former United States Surgeon General

> "The Genetics in Clinical Practice: A Team Approach training program has been the gold standard
> of interactive multimedia programs for nearly a decade."
>

On adoption, from a national hazmat trainer: VTRA in use "from Washington to Wisconsin, from
Michigan to Houston, from California to North Carolina," with a copy secured for every hazmat
team in Connecticut. Testers included the FBI, the Indiana Department of Homeland Security and
Guilford County Emergency Services in North Carolina.

## Why it is here

Most web portfolios show sites. This one shows a decade of selling complex, accredited,
federally funded products to professionals who do not buy on enthusiasm — and the marketing
sites that had to carry that.

It is also the clearest answer to a question institutional clients ask without saying it out
loud: does this person understand how our world works? Grant compliance, federal reporting,
accreditation bodies, and clinicians who need a certificate at the end are not things you pick
up from a brief.
