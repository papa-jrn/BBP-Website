## The situation

Claremont Community Media Center — Claremont Community Television — is the 501(c)(3) that
runs public, education and government programming for Claremont, New Hampshire and Sullivan
County. Two cable channels: Channel 8 carries school board meetings, local programmes and
Sunday worship; Channel 10 carries city council, zoning, planning, conservation commission
and county commissioner meetings.

Public access organisations have a particular problem. They produce an enormous amount of
genuinely important civic video — the meetings where a town's decisions actually get made —
and most of it is hard to use after broadcast. A resident who wants to know what was said
about one school budget line faces a three-hour recording and no obvious way in.

## The honest disclosure

Bee Balm's principal is also this organisation's Executive Director. That is stated plainly
rather than buried: this was not an arm's-length commission. What it does mean is unusually
complete knowledge of the operation — the broadcast workflow, the municipal relationships,
the funding constraints and what residents actually ask for — informing every decision.

## What Bee Balm built

**A site that leads with watching.** Live streams for both channels are the first thing on
the page, because that is what most visitors came for. The video library is browsable and
searchable rather than being an archive you need to already know your way around.

**A publishing pipeline for contributors.** The more durable contribution is less visible.
The site is set up so that a developer who is not staff can make changes, review them and
publish them safely — version control through Git, deployment through Cloudflare. No
handing files to someone, no editing live, no single person as the bottleneck for every
change.

That matters because small nonprofits rarely have a second developer and almost never have
a safe way for an outside contributor to help. Giving a volunteer a path to ship is the
difference between occasional help and sustained contribution.

## Where that pipeline paid off

The School Board Meeting Navigator — the tool that lets residents find a specific item
inside the school board meeting library instead of scrubbing through a three-hour file —
was **built by Kevin Tyson**, using LLM-assisted development. Bee Balm's part was polishing
it and providing the workflow through which it reaches the live site.

It is worth naming because it demonstrates the point of the infrastructure. A community
media organisation with a $140K budget got a genuinely useful piece of civic software
because a capable contributor had a safe route to publish, and because AI-assisted
development put the build within reach of one person's available time.

## Results

Over the period, monthly programming output tripled from roughly 20 to over 60 community,
educational and municipal programmes, and digital reach across platforms grew by more than
1,300% in a single year.

The web work sits alongside a full modernisation of the station: 4K production standards,
two internal studios, a mobile production unit, and the streaming system the site depends
on.

## Why this generalises

Every municipality, school district and public body in the region faces the same obligation
to record and publish meetings, usually on a budget that assumes no technical staff at all.
The pattern here — live streaming, a usable library, and a contribution workflow that lets
whoever is available help without breaking the site — applies directly to any of them.
