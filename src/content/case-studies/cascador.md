## The situation

Cascador runs a selective accelerator for growth-stage founders building companies in
Africa. Its ScaleUp programme takes a cohort through leadership development, strategy work
and investor access, and alumni become eligible for a catalytic fund deploying up to $5M a
year.

An organisation like this puts unusual demands on a website. It has to persuade three
different audiences at once — founders deciding whether to apply, funders deciding whether
to back the fund, and partner institutions deciding whether to lend their name — and it has
to be rebuilt in part every year as a new cohort is selected, alumni accumulate and the
programme itself evolves.

## What the work involved

This was a long engagement rather than a project. Over six years the site carried the
organisation through repeated changes of programme structure, cohort intake, funding model
and positioning, each of which needed the information architecture revisited rather than
just the copy swapped.

The build sits on WordPress with the Divi builder — the right call for a client whose staff
needed to update cohort and alumni content themselves between engagements. But the design
ambition ran past what the page builder does on its own, and a meaningful amount of custom
CSS and JavaScript sits underneath the layouts to achieve the intended look and the
repeating cohort and alumni patterns.

## Why it mattered

Annual cohort programmes live or die on application volume and quality. The site is the
front door for founders across a continent deciding whether this is a programme worth
applying to, and the credibility signal for the institutional partners whose logos sit at
the bottom of the page.

## How the engagement ended

Cascador has since moved its working group to a team based in Nigeria, closer to the
founders it serves. That is a sensible decision for the organisation and had nothing to do
with the work — but it is worth recording honestly, because it is the kind of thing that
happens to a concentrated client relationship and is the reason Bee Balm now structures
engagements differently.
