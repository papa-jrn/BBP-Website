## The situation

Lee Brandon is a strength and conditioning specialist with a record most practitioners would
struggle to believe. First female assistant strength coach in the NFL. Three-time World Long
Drive Champion. Olympic coach across Los Angeles 1984, London 2012, Rio 2016 and Tokyo 2020.
Inventor of a patented training system that made Oprah's Top 20 Inventions list. Forty years
in the industry, with emeritus registration and credentials across strength conditioning,
golf performance and physical therapy.

That is the problem. A record that broad is genuinely hard to communicate. Presented as a
list it reads as either overwhelming or, perversely, less credible — the sheer range invites
disbelief. And none of it answers the question a prospective client actually arrives with,
which is *can you help me specifically*.

## The work

**Content first, not layout first.** The main contribution here was written, not visual.
Lee's background was researched and rewritten into a structure that leads with what she
does for a client, supports it with the credentials that make the claim believable, and
keeps the certifications accessible rather than making them the pitch.

**Explaining the credential, not just displaying it.** CSCS, RSCC\*E and NSPT mean a great
deal inside the profession and nothing at all to a prospective client. The site explains
what a Certified Strength and Conditioning Specialist actually is and why the distinction
matters, converting an acronym into a reason to trust.

**A proposition that fits on one line.** *Do what you love — just longer.* It states the
outcome a client is buying rather than the credentials of the person selling it. The
credentials then back it up.

**Structure around the offer.** One-to-one training leads, with the inventions, media
appearances and endorsements arranged to support rather than compete with it.

## Why this one is here

Most web projects are a build. This one was mostly a thinking and writing job — working out
what a client is actually selling, then making the case for it in words.

That is the harder and more valuable half of the work, and it is the half that separates a
website from a website that produces enquiries. It is also the capability most studios
quietly leave to the client, which is why so many sites launch with placeholder copy and
never recover.
