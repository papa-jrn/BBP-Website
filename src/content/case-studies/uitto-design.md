## The situation

Uitto Design Workshop is a residential architecture practice led by Greg Uitto, working on
new builds, additions and extensive renovations — turning vacant lots and awkward existing
houses into considered homes. The practice competes on design quality and on a
collaborative, client-first philosophy.

For an architect, the website *is* the portfolio. A prospective client is deciding whether
this is someone whose taste they trust with the most expensive thing they will ever own.
The bar for visual execution is therefore set by the client's own profession — a site that
looks merely competent actively undermines the pitch.

## The challenge

The design direction came from a high-end visual standard, and the gap between that vision
and what a page builder produces by default is exactly where most of this kind of project
fails. Matching it meant writing a significant amount of custom CSS and JavaScript beneath
the builder layouts rather than assembling stock modules — custom typographic treatment,
bespoke layout behaviour, and image presentation that lets the architecture carry the page.

The underlying structure also had to hold a growing body of work. Each project — Cedar Home,
Cedar Garage, Grape Pop Top, 19th Street, Kneale Road, Oak Avenue — needed its own page in a
consistent pattern the practice could extend as new builds completed.

## Approach

**Implement the design, don't approximate it.** Where the builder could not produce the
intended result, it was extended with custom code rather than the design being compromised
to fit the tool.

**Structure for growth.** A repeatable project page pattern means adding the next completed
build is a content task, not a design exercise.

**Let the work lead.** Architecture photography is the argument. Layout and typography are
there to stay out of its way.

## Worth noting

Video is a natural fit for architectural work — walkthroughs and site films do something
stills cannot — and that capability sits in-house at Bee Balm alongside the web work. It was
not part of this engagement, partly a matter of geography, but it is available where a
practice wants its work shown moving.
